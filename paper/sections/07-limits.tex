\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Small test sets.}
With 40--80 tasks per workflow, many variants differ by less than the noise, as the full workflow's
disagreement with its own re-run shows. Pairwise agreement over \emph{all} pairs is therefore
bounded well below one for any evaluation, full or miniature; we report clear pairs and the noise
ceiling for this reason, and we read every miniature against that ceiling rather than against
perfection.

\paragraph{One model family per workflow.}
All Gemini runs use one small model, and only W1 was also run on a second family. Larger models may
follow edits more faithfully, which would widen the gaps between variants and make every
evaluation, full or miniature, easier.

\paragraph{The judge is an LLM.}
Scores outside exact matches and every failure reason come from a blind LLM judge (Claude), not from
people. Reasons are drawn from a fixed taxonomy per workflow; a different taxonomy or judge could
change the reasoning-agreement numbers. All judge prompts and raw answers are released.

\paragraph{Free tools understate the savings of simulation.}
Our live tools (Wikipedia, a local database) are free and fast, so tool simulation adds LLM cost
instead of removing tool cost. In workflows whose tools are paid, slow, rate-limited or have side
effects, the same operators save far more; their validity, which is what we measure, does not
depend on the tools' price.

\paragraph{Miniatures can be overfitted.}
A team that optimises against one miniature for long enough may learn its quirks (a simulator's
habits, a stub's blind spots), a form of Goodhart's law \citep{manheim2018categorizing}. Periodic
checks on the full workflow, which our mutant check and noise ceiling make cheap to interpret,
remain necessary.
