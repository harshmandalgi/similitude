\section{Conclusion}

A miniature of an agent workflow can make the same decisions as the full workflow at a fraction
of its cost, but only some miniatures can. The rule that held across our three workflows is short:
keep real what you change, replay what you did not change, fake the tools rather than the agent's
thinking, and ground the fakes in what the real tools returned. Shortcuts that let the model imagine
the results of its own actions (merged calls, stubbed sub-agents, a whole pipeline in one call)
measure the model's knowledge, not the workflow's behaviour, and fail exactly where the change under
test acts through the tools.

Two lessons concern evaluation itself. First, at realistic test-set sizes the full evaluation
settles only a few decisions, and sometimes none: a miniature must be read against the full
workflow's agreement with itself, or a lossless miniature will be blamed for the full workflow's
noise. Second, keeping decisions does not guarantee keeping diagnoses; a miniature used to decide
\emph{what to fix} should be checked on the judge's reasons, not only on scores.

The natural next step is to use validated miniatures where they pay most: in optimisation loops
that evaluate hundreds of candidates, with the full workflow reserved for the few that survive.
