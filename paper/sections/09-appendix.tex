\section{Workflows in detail}
\label{app:workflows}

\paragraph{W1: Wikipedia question answering.}
One tool-calling agent (at most 10 steps) with two live tools over the English Wikipedia API:
\texttt{search\_wikipedia} (up to five titles with snippets) and \texttt{read\_page} (3{,}000
characters of plain text at a time, from an offset). Tasks: 80 test and 20 development questions
sampled from the HotpotQA distractor validation split \citep{@hotpotqa}, keeping its natural
mix (64 bridge, 16 comparison). The agent ends with \texttt{FINAL ANSWER: <answer>}; a normalised
exact match is correct by rule, anything else goes to the judge. Base prompt:

\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
You answer questions using Wikipedia. You have two tools: search\_wikipedia finds pages, and
read\_page reads a page. Questions often need facts from two pages: find the first fact, then use
it to find the second. Do not answer from memory: check the facts on Wikipedia. When you are done,
reply with one line: FINAL ANSWER: <answer>. The answer must be short: a name, date, number, or
yes/no. No explanation.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{W2: $\tau$-bench retail.}
The retail domain of $\tau$-bench \citep{@taubench}, vendored unchanged (tools, data, policy,
tasks; MIT licence). The agent's system prompt is the shop policy, optionally preceded by a
preamble (the edit variants change only the preamble); it has the 16 retail tools and at most 30
steps. The customer is simulated by an LLM with $\tau$-bench's own user prompt, verbatim. Tasks:
the first 40 test tasks. Pass/fail is $\tau$-bench's check (database state and required facts),
computed by replaying the agent's write calls on a fresh database (\S\ref{sec:method}).

\paragraph{W3: research pipeline.}
A planner splits a FRAMES question \citep{@frames} into two to four sub-questions; for each,
a researcher (a tool-calling agent with the W1 tools, at most six steps, seeing earlier notes)
returns notes; a writer combines the notes into an answer; a reviewer flags concrete errors; a
reviser applies them if asked. A side branch writes a two-sentence log summary that nobody scores.
Tasks: 80 test and 10 development questions sampled from FRAMES among those that need two or three
Wikipedia pages and no table reading.

\paragraph{Models.}
Every node runs on \texttt{gemini-3.5-flash-lite} through Vertex AI, with
\texttt{gemini-3.1-flash-lite} as the cheaper model (\op{B3}) and tool simulator (\op{C1}), and
\texttt{gemini-3.8-flash} for the stronger-model variants. A first W1 ground truth on
\texttt{gpt-5.4-nano} is used for the cross-family comparison.

\section{Variants}
\label{app:variants}
Each workflow has a base, \emph{edits}, \emph{mutants} and \emph{model} variants. Edits are
plausible improvements written by a separate Claude Code instance (Claude Opus 5.5) shown the base
prompts and five development questions, asked for one idea per edit. Mutants are deliberate
breakages written by hand (for example: answer from memory; read at most one page; a two-step cap;
the policy without its authentication rule). Model variants swap the focus node's model.

\section{Judging}
\label{app:judge}
The judge is Claude Sonnet~5.5 at medium effort through Claude Code, with no tools, no project
files and no memory, in batches of 25 runs under random codes; the OpenAI W1 runs were judged by
Claude Opus~5.5 at high effort. Failure taxonomies:
W1: \emph{gave up, retrieval miss, wrong hop, reasoning error, unsupported, format, gold issue}.
W2: \emph{auth failure, wrong action, missing action, extra action, policy violation, missing info,
transferred, user-simulator issue}.
W3: \emph{gave up, plan error, retrieval miss, synthesis error, reasoning error, unsupported,
format, gold issue}.
All judge prompts and every raw judge answer are released with the code.
