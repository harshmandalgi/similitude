A full evaluation of an agent workflow is slow and costly, so teams test few changes. We ask if a
small copy of the workflow, a \emph{miniature}, can make the same decisions as the full
evaluation. A decision is the choice of the better of two variants of the workflow. We built
\sys{}, a system that applies thirteen shortcuts to the model calls and tool calls of a workflow,
with no change to its code.

We tested sixteen miniatures of three workflows: a Wikipedia agent, a $\tau$-bench shop agent and
a five-role research pipeline. The best miniatures kept 90--100\% of the clear decisions of the
full evaluation. Simulated tools kept 18--19 of 20 clear decisions for the Wikipedia agent and all
8 for the research planner. Replay of unchanged steps and a quarter of the tasks each cost about
one fifth of a full evaluation. We also found two limits: merged calls that imagine tool results,
and simulators that must replace private data. From these results, we give six rules to build a
valid miniature.
