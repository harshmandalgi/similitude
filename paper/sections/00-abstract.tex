A full evaluation of an agent workflow is slow and costly, so teams test few changes. We ask if a
small copy of the workflow, a \emph{miniature}, can make the same decisions as the full
evaluation. A decision is the choice of the better of two variants of the workflow. We built
\sys{}\codefootnote, a system that applies thirteen shortcuts to the model calls and tool calls of a
workflow. The workflow sends its calls through one runtime and needs no other change.

We tested sixteen miniatures of three workflows: a Wikipedia agent, a $\tau$-bench shop agent and
a five-role research pipeline. Simulated tools kept 18--19 of 20 clear decisions for the Wikipedia
agent and all 8 for the research planner, close to a second full run. Our real tools are free, so
these miniatures cost about as much as the full workflow. A quarter of the tasks kept 85\% of the
clear decisions at one fifth of the cost. A second full run, the noise ceiling, showed when the full
evaluation itself was noise.

We also found two limits. Merged calls that imagine the tool results missed every broken variant,
and a simulator cannot replace a tool that reads hidden data. From these results, we give six rules
to build a valid miniature.
